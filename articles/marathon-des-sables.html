The Marathon des Sables — the toughest foot-race on Earth
January 2021

Yes, you saw that right. Those are people. Running. Voluntarily. In the desert. The Marathon des Sables — the Marathon of the Sands — is a snapshot of one of the toughest endurance races on earth.

The MdS, as it is abbreviated, is the ultimate dream for many runners. Over the course of seven days, runners cover around 250 kilometres in the Moroccan desert, carrying their own food, drinks and supplies, and sleeping at night in communal tents.

Every day, runners have to cover a specific distance ranging from 15 km to 80 kilometres, with the longest stage at around 82.2 kilometres, after which runners get a rest day. Runners carry backpacks weighing around 6.4–15 kg, including food and mandatory supplies. Water is provided by the organisers: 1.5 litres in the morning, a further 1.5–4.5 litres throughout the day at checkpoints, and 4.5 litres at the end of the day for meals, drinking and washing.

The 450-strong team of race staff operates sophisticated facilities for waste disposal, medical check-ups, live broadcasting and living arrangements. Runners battle 50-degree Celsius temperatures, burning sand, rocky terrain and indigenous animal species. Sand storms and high-speed winds can also cause temporary halts.

The race organizers operate Operation Desert Clean — a sophisticated system of leaving no waste behind. Runners can be heavily fined for even leaving a bottle cap in the desert.

To the ordinary person, the race seems like something only mad men and women would undertake. For runners, however, this ultimate challenge is a lifelong dream — the rarest diamond in their crown of running achievements. Crossing the finish line signifies that you were able to beat your own demons, conquer your thirst, and push your body to extremes that one wouldn't have imagined were humanly possible. It is a testament to what sports always tell us — that anything and everything is possible as long as you put your mind to it.
