The rise of women's football
May 2021

The 16th of May 2021 saw FC Barcelona achieve a historic feat when FC Barcelona Femení lifted the UEFA Women's Champions League by thrashing WSL champions Chelsea 4-0. Barcelona is now the first team to win both the female (2021) and male (1991–92, 2005–06, 2008–09, 2010–11, 2014–15) editions of the UEFA Champions League.

FCB Femení had already been crowned domestic league champions on May 9, 2021. The team has not dropped a single point this season, winning all 26 of their domestic games, and have become domestic league champions for a record sixth time. Number seven Jennifer Hermoso finished as the top scorer in the Champions League. (Forgive the fangirling — this is my favourite team.)

The win comes at a perfect time, when women's football is getting the hype it deserves. The revolution ignited with the 2019 Women's World Cup, which attracted a combined audience of 1.12 billion viewers. Several sponsors have since started signing deals with clubs. UEFA Women's Football will now be sponsored by Visa, Pepsi, Just Eat Takeaway.com, Hublot and most recently, Euronics Group — covering the Women's Champions League, Women's Euro 2022, and multiple other competitions.

Most prominent among female players is the outspoken USWNT captain Megan Rapinoe, who shot to fame during the 2019 World Cup and has been a staunch advocate of equal pay between male and female athletes. The 2019 Women's World Cup final was played in front of a sold-out crowd of 57,900 at Groupama Stadium, France.

A RunRepeat Survey has shown that viewership could increase by 300%–350% if female football could be made more accessible via TV. In 2020, the US National Women's Soccer League broke its viewership records with an increase of around 300%.

Equal pay, equal respect and equal opportunity are basic needs. It shows in the numbers. It is a human right and every human deserves it.
