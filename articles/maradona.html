A tribute: Diego Maradona
November 2020

25th November 2020 is a date that no football fan will ever forget. What started off as an ordinary Wednesday turned into a day of mourning for football fans around the world as news trickled out from Buenos Aires that a myocardial infarction had claimed one of the greatest minds ever seen in football — that of Diego Armando Maradona.

Maradona — or “El Pibe de Oro” (“The Golden Boy”) — was a regular face in the newspapers both for actions on and off the green grass of the pitch. There is one thing that no tribute dedicated to Maradona will ever miss out on — the “Hand of God” during the Argentina-England quarter final of the 1986 World Cup.

Known even by non-football fans, the “Hand of God” was a defining moment in the history of Maradona's life — the moment when he was etched into every Argentine's heart forever. The goal did not even come close to being the best Maradona ever scored (the one he scored four minutes later, also known as the Goal of the Century, was) — but it marked an important milestone given the volatile history between England and Argentina, not only on the pitch, but also geo-politically.

A brief timeline of major events between the two nations before the 1986 match:

  • 1966: The World Cup quarter-final saw Argentine captain Antonio Rattín inexplicably sent off. The Argentines called it “el robo del siglo” — “theft of the century.” Tensions heightened further when England manager Alf Ramsey reportedly called the South American side “animals.”
  • 1982: The Falklands War — a conflict between the UK and Argentina over the ownership of the Falkland Islands — ended with England regaining possession, but not without significant Argentine casualties.

The victory of Argentina over England on that hot day in Mexico City cemented Maradona into the hearts of Argentines everywhere. As captain, he led Argentina to the World Championship in 1986 — the last captain to ever lead Argentina to a World Cup.

To many, Maradona represented the perfect underdog story. He brought the stuff of fairy-tales to the stadium, moving with the ball seemingly glued to his foot, full of tricks and flair that made him mesmerising to watch. Everywhere he went, he brought trophies and magic — he turned squares in cities into party-zones and made every fan's dreams come true.

To many, Maradona was a living legend — proof of excellence and success, a seemingly ordinary person capable of extraordinary feats. There wasn't a shred of doubt that his “Player of the Century” award, shared jointly with Pelé, was the right one.

Earth may have lost an icon of football, but Heaven just gained their best footballer.

Rest in peace, Diego Armando Maradona.
