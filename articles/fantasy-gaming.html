The rise of the fantasy gaming industry in India
May 2021

Dream11 — the fantasy gaming platform — shot to mainstream fame after being declared the title sponsors of the 2020 Indian Premier League — arguably India's biggest sporting event. Interest in the platform shot up to its peak in the first week of IPL, reaching their target audience in its entirety at a time when sports sentiment was running high.

Fantasy gaming is a form of sport in which gamers choose their own squads using real-world players. They then get points based on the real-life performance of the players they have chosen. Fantasy gaming came to India in 2012, when Dream11 launched a cricket gaming freemium model for fans.

Dream11 was conceived in 2008, based loosely on the uber-popular Fantasy Premier League. At that time, it operated on a free-to-play platform with ad revenue. In 2012, they switched to a freemium model based around daily fantasy sports, removing ads and introducing the ability for users to put money on their teams. The money collected from users in a particular contest would go into a pool, which funded the prizes of winners after the deduction of a service fee.

In 2021, the fantasy sports space is far more crowded — MyTeam11, My11Circle, Howzat, Halaplay, 11Wicket, MyFab11, MPL Fantasy Cricket and Ballebaazi being just some of the platforms. According to a FICCI-EY report, online gaming was the fastest growing segment in 2019, “on the back of transaction-based games, mainly fantasy sports, and a 31% growth in online gamers.” The Indian online gaming segment is expected to reach ₹187 billion by 2022.

The aggressive marketing led by well-known faces like MS Dhoni, Sourav Ganguly and Virat Kohli during IPL and ICC WC competitions, combined with the rise of affordable and accessible data, have been the biggest causes for the growth of this industry.

Is this even legal in India, where gambling and betting are punishable by law? The answer is yes — fantasy gaming falls under the banner of “game of skill” rather than “game of chance.” However, states like Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Odisha, Tamil Nadu, Nagaland and Telangana do not allow the use of Dream11, as this falls under state rather than central jurisdiction.

The future of fantasy and online sports looks promising in India, and would be a very interesting industry to follow.
