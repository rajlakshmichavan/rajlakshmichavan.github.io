Project Iceman: Limitations are Perceptions
November 2020

At 5:30 a.m. on the 22nd of February 2020, Anders Hofman plunged into the water and began to swim. After completing 3.9 kilometres, he dried himself and got ready to bike 180 kilometres. After that, he completed the marathon distance of 42.2 kilometres. Why is this particular triathlon so special?

It was conducted in the most desolate, the harshest and the coldest conditions known to man — the continent of Antarctica.

After an astonishing 72:54:09 hours — delayed by 27 hours due to a 150 kmph blizzard on the glacier — Anders Hofman became the first man to complete an Ironman triathlon in Antarctica.

The idea came to Hofman in 2017 when he watched a video of Nick Jacobsen kite-jumping off a 321-metre high helipad of the Burj Al Arab in Dubai. He wanted to do something similar — perhaps something where he wouldn't be the best, but where he would be the first. He found that Antarctica was the only continent where the Ironman hadn't been completed yet. Apart from the deathly cold and inhabitable conditions, there were other dangers: leopard seals in the waters, equipment suited to both glacier and sea, the funding, the team, the training. More than anything, however, the biggest challenge he faced was dealing with the “this is crazy”s, the “this is impossible”s, and the “you're going to fail”s.

He publicly announced his attempt on June 13, 2018. To prepare, he ran the Polar Circle Marathon in Greenland, completed the northernmost triathlon in Svalbard, and finally, in 2020, completed the Iceman. During the actual process itself, all Hofman could think of was how cold it was, how much more he had left to do, how stupid the whole thing was. He spent 27 hours without sleeping, cycling through a ceaselessly white landscape. The first signs of hypothermia hit him as soon as he climbed out of the water.

Every one of us has dreams. And yet, we don't work towards many of them — just because we think we won't succeed, because it's too hard, because no one's done it before, because we might fail. Even if we do take the plunge, failure at any stage makes us want to give up entirely. You need to find that one true reason in your heart that drives you to go for it.

Nothing is impossible. Dreams are meant to be achieved.

Limitations are perceptions.
